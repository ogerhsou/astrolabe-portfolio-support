of every trade.

Start with your opening cash balance, or load the sample: 10,000 opening cash, a 1,000 purchase, 440 in sale proceeds and a 20 dividend.

This tool checks arithmetic from the entries you provide. It cannot detect an omitted or incorrect transaction on its own.

How cash reconciliation works.

Expected closing cash = opening cash + deposits + sale proceeds + cash income − withdrawals − purchases − cash charges.

For the sample, 10,000 + 440 + 20 − 1,000 = 9,460. If your recorded cash says 10,460, it is 1,000 higher than these entries explain. A missing purchase is one thing to check; it is not the only possible explanation.

Keep the entries consistent.

  • Check one account and one currency at a time. Do not add USD and EUR amounts together.
  • Use one cash measure and date basis throughout. A settled-cash balance and a buying-power figure can differ. Match your statement’s treatment of unsettled trades.
  • Include fees once. If your purchase totals or sale proceeds already include them, do not enter those fees again.
  • Use dividends actually credited to cash. A reinvested dividend also has a corresponding purchase; include both if they appear as separate cash movements.
  • Price changes in your holdings do not directly change cash. Transfers between accounts change the cash in each account, even if your combined net worth stays the same.
